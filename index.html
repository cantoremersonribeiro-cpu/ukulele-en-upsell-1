WHAT USUALLY HAPPENS

Most people stop strumming while their fingers look for the next chord. The beat breaks, the song stops, and they start the bar again.

They practice the same change over and over, lifting every finger every time, and decide that smooth changes are something other people are born with.

WHY THAT IS THE WRONG STARTING POINT

Chord changes follow simple rules. Many changes keep at least one finger exactly where it is, and the rest move as one shape. Once you know that, a smooth change is a method, not a gift.

The Ukulele Chord Change Map gives you that method in five steps — with three changes worked out finger by finger, so you can see it done before you try it yourself.

Your 5-Step Chord Change Path
The same five steps for every change
1
Find the pivotLook for the finger that stays on the same string and fret in both chords. It never lifts.Easy
2
Move as one shapeLift the other fingers together and land them together, like a stamp, not one by one.Easy
3
Change on the last up strumLet the last up strum of the bar ring on open strings while your fingers travel. Beat 1 lands on time.Easy
4
The one-minute drillCount how many clean changes you make in one minute and write it down. The number climbs.Moderate
5
Add the strumPut the change back inside a pattern from the Strumming Map, starting with steady downs.Moderate
